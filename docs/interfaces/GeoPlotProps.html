    Optionalaggregates

    aggregates?: GeoAggregateOptions

    How the aggregates are drawn (in the aggregates and auto modes): as circles, pies, or cells, sized and colored by what, and with what keys (see GeoAggregateOptions). The object itself, and its ranges, can be written inline; its functions (colorScheme, groupColors) should keep their identity from one render to the next (defined outside the component, or memoized), as radiusOf and colorOf should, since a new one redraws the plot.

    • Defined in plots/GeoPlot.tsx:310

    OptionalautoRollups

    autoRollups?: string[]

    The roll-ups that the auto mode chooses among, by name: roll-ups of the same events whose cells are of different sizes (e.g. byGridCell(10), byGridCell(1), byGridCell(0.1)), so that it shows, at each zoom, the coarsest whose cells span at most clusterDistance pixels -- or the events, once even the finest's cells span more. Defaults to all of the data source's roll-ups whose cells are of a size (see GeoBinning's cellSize).

    • Defined in plots/GeoPlot.tsx:291

    OptionalautoRotate

    autoRotate?: number

    When set, the view turns westward (the earth spins eastward) at this many degrees per second, pausing while the view is dragged and while an event is hovered. Defaults to no rotation. The plot redraws up to 60 times a second while it turns, so with many tens of thousands of events held, show a roll-up's aggregates instead, or keep fewer events (the data source's dropDataAfter). Not in the "shift" view mode.

    Optionalbasemap

    basemap?: GeoMapData

    The land (or any other areas) to draw under the events, as GeoJSON (e.g. the land features of a world atlas) -- or versions of it at different levels of detail, each drawn from its own zoom in (see GeoMapData). The plot doesn't come with a map, so that the application chooses its level of detail. Drawn filled, in landColor.

    OptionalfitTo

    fitTo?: GeoPermissibleObjects

    A shape (GeoJSON, e.g. a country, or a state) that the view frames when the plot first shows, and after its view is reset: centered on the shape, and zoomed in until it fills the plot area, less fitPadding. Changing it re-frames the view; and until the user moves the view, the view keeps the shape framed as the plot's size changes.     OptionalhoveredSeries

    hoveredSeries?: "isolate" | "highlight"

    What hovering a series does -- its entry in the legend, or, on the map, one of its events (or an aggregate's wedge whose group is named for it): show that series' events alone (isolate, the default -- a map's series often overlap at the same places), or draw its events over the others', at full opacity, so that they stand out, with the others a little fainter, but still readable (highlight). Either way, the events and their flashes are affected (in isolate, the other series' events can't be hovered until the mouse leaves the event); and the aggregates' pies, when the hovered series names one of their groups, draw its wedges at full opacity, and the other groups' a little fainter.

    • Defined in plots/GeoPlot.tsx:351

    Optionallayers

    layers?: GeoLayer[]

    More of the map, drawn over the basemap, in order, and under the borders (see GeoLayer): e.g. a country's states, colored by a value of each (a choropleth, with a fill function of the feature), its counties (only once zoomed in, see minZoom), its roads, or its lakes -- each with its own colors, line width, and opacity, versions by zoom, and labels. Only the features that might show in the view are drawn, and the map is clipped to the plot area, so that a detailed map, zoomed in, draws quickly.

    Optionalmode

    mode?: "auto" | "events" | "aggregates"

    What the plot shows: each event (events, the default); the aggregates of one of the data source's roll-ups (aggregates, see rollup, and the GeoDataSource's rollups); or, depending on the zoom, a roll-up whose cells are about as small on the screen as what can be told apart, or, zoomed in far enough, the events (auto, see autoRollups and clusterDistance) -- so that a busy map, seen from afar, shows clusters, which come apart as it's zoomed in. The auto mode's clusters, like its events, are of the series the chart's seriesFilter shows; a roll-up shown in the aggregates mode takes the events it says it takes (see its filter).

    OptionalviewMode

    viewMode?: "shift" | "turn"

    How the view moves: by turning the earth (turn, the default) -- a globe turns under a drag, every way, and a flat map turns east and west, and shifts north and south -- or by shifting the map, like a picture (shift): fitted to fitTo (or the whole sphere), a drag slides it, and the wheel zooms it about the mouse, the projection's own rotation left as it is (e.g. a conic map of one state, set up for it). A projection that can't be turned -- a composite of several, like albersUsa (the lower 48 states, with Alaska and Hawaii moved in) -- is always shown in the shift mode, and needs fitTo (e.g. the United States), since it can't show the whole sphere.
