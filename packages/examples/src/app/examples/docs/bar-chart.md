### the bar chart

The bar chart shows each series as a bar – but not just the bar you're thinking of. For each series, it can show the *current* value, and also how the value has behaved over time: its minimum and maximum, and its mean. And it can show those statistics two ways – over the series' whole lifetime, and over just the recent window of data.

So where the scatter chart shows you the *history* of each series, the bar chart shows you a *summary* of each series, as it changes. That's handy when you have lots of series, and care more about "where is each one now, and where has it been" than about the exact path it took.

The example has fifty series – the same "HC 1" through "HC 50" neurons as the raster chart – each with a value between -1 and 1. The data is generated by a function designed for no better reason than to make the bars dance &#x1F57A;.

The chart's x-axes are *ordinal* (category) axes, one category per series, with the labels rotated so that they fit. The y-axes are the values.

For each series, the bar chart draws up to five things on top of each other:

- **value** – a line at the series' current (latest) value
- **min/max** – a bar spanning the smallest to the largest value the series has had over its lifetime
- **mean** – a line at the series' mean value over its lifetime
- **win min/max** – a (narrower) bar spanning the smallest to the largest value within the current window of data
- **win mean** – a line at the mean value within the current window of data

The "window" is the data the chart is currently holding – which is set by the drop-after drop-down in the common controls. So with "Drop after 10 s", the windowed statistics cover the last 10 seconds.

The tracker on this chart is horizontal, and labels the value on the y-axis – which makes it easy to compare the bars.

The bar chart has a *second* control bar, to the right of the first, with a checkbox for each of the five things above: **value**, **min/max**, **mean**, **win min/max**, and **win mean**. Its header shows a symbol for each one (`x` for the value, `x̄` for the mean, and so on) that lights up when it's shown. Turn some off to see the rest more clearly. *Wired to:* the plot's `showValueLines`, `showMinMaxBars`, `showMeanValueLines`, `showWindowedMinMaxBars`, and `showWindowedMeanValueLines` properties.

The first control bar also has an **auto-scale y-axes** checkbox, which works just like the scatter chart's: when it's on, the y-axes start out narrower (−0.5 to 0.5), and grow to fit what's shown of the bars on screen – the min/max bars, the means, and the values, whichever are shown – but never past −1.1 to 1.1. Like the scatter chart's, an axis only ever grows, keeps its growth when you switch tabs, and starts over when the data is replaced. *Wired to:* the y-axes' `autoScale`, `autoScaleMin`, `autoScaleMax`, `autoScaledDomain`, and `onAutoScale` properties.

#### where the code lives

The example (yours to copy, change, or ignore):
- `src/app/examples/StreamingBarChart.tsx` – the two control bars, and the chart itself
- `src/app/examples/appstate/barChartStore.ts` – the example's state, including its data source and zoom
- `src/app/examples/dataproviders/randomOrdinalData.ts` – the dancing-bars data generator

The library (what you'd use in your own application):
- `<Chart>`, `<OrdinalAxis>`, `<ContinuousAxis>`, `<BarPlot>`, `<Tracker>`, `<Tooltip>` with `<BarPlotTooltipContent>`
- `OrdinalDataSource` – holds the series, and keeps both the lifetime and windowed statistics up to date (even while the chart isn't showing)
